The Seven Minutes of Regulus Black

by ThePikovayaDama

It is said, that after the physical death of a person, their brain remains alive for approximately seven minutes, to replay their happiest memories, or to show them an imagined version of whatever afterlife they believed in. In a way of final comfort before the end, maybe, or perhaps to see someone for the last time when you did not get to. Regulus' Seven Minutes was James.

Upon entering the Horcrux cave as he bid farewell to the world and his life, he thought of his friends, of Barty and Evan, of Pandora and Dorcas, their Hogwart's years together, of all the unsaid goodbyes and all of the undone things which they never got to do as kids and now never will. Regulus has forbidden himself from thinking about anything else. The locks on some memories in his mind could give an honest challenge to those on the doors of some rooms in the Black household - because what he was about to do was no place for those feelings, feelings that could oh so easily make him forget everything he came here for. Even though nothing could be saved now.

Yet he was dying with a call for his brother on his lips in the form of a ripped, raw scream, which was the last thing he ever said and the last physical sound his body produced, when the bloodless hands pulled him into the liquified, hungry darkness of the water. An echo behind a locked door, reflecting Regulus' childhood, reflected in the emptied, crumbling halls of his mind. A last call to his naive instinct as a child to call for Sirius when he was in pain, because Sirius would make it stop. He always did. That is why the lock to Sirius' door in his mind was one of the strongest, because of how much it kept sealed. How he mended his wounds and took away his pain, even if it meant distracting their parents from Regulus and receiving more pain for him in return - Shh, Reggie, it's all alright now - hey, watch this, I'm gonna go downstairs and explode Dr Flibustier's fireworks under the table, Mum's gonna go nuts!

But when the water filling his lungs has pushed out the last of his oxygen, and the blackness has pulled closed and shut above his head, when hands that were digging their claws into his skin and ripping him apart into bloody shreds started to drag him towards the bottom of the lake, the only thing that Regulus saw was he, whom Regulus has not permitted himself to think about at all, yet did not have the restrain to any longer. The final, most secure lock came off hitting the floor with a thud, the door was torn into wooden shards. The only one whom he saw was James.

Sunlight. That kind of warmth, after which the familiar coldness that you thought you got used to seems like pure ice. Hazel eyes crinkled with bubbling laughter behind crooked glasses. The taste of shared sweets in summer mornings, and even sweeter words in the summer nights. The ringing church bells of an unreal, never happening wedding day, which neither him, nor James would ever get to experience, yet Regulus hears them more clearly than he has ever heard his own voice. Stupid, lovely, absurdly beautiful James.

Their magically bright future made of light, and caring hands that touched him gently, while everything around Regulus is the dark, and unforgiving, cruel pale fingers eager to claw his silenced heart out and make him one of them. Ones he could not even feel anymore, because the rest of his body was no longer physically alive.

And this is how, with a final dream about a distant and unreachable Sun, in the ink black of the water amongst bloodless hands, one Star has drowned, went out and forever lost its shine. Those were the Seven Minutes for Regulus Black.

Seven minutes for seven years for which they known each other. Seven minutes for all the years that they will not. Seven minutes for the number of times James asked, begged Regulus with his lovely, tear-bright eyes that are usually so soft to leave his family, leave that household, leave for him and seven minutes for the number of times that Regulus had refused , even though the only thing he wanted to say was yes. Just seven minutes for the one other version of reality where he did. Seven minutes for the number of freckles on James' face. Seven for the seven hours into the midnight and morning that James spent comforting him on the night that Regulus got the Mark, carefully holding his left forearm with red, scratching marks from his fingernails and seven minutes for the number of times James told him he loved him before Regulus actually believed it. Seven more times than he should have had to, actually, because oh, what a fool Regulus was to doubt him - James Potter could never be ingenuine when it came to his feelings. Maybe it was just something in him that wanted to hear it again and again, without knowing that if Regulus needed him to, James would be more than glad to just as well repeat it in every conversation for the rest of his life. Seven minutes for the alternative, parallel universe where he did. Seven, for a short life that they had, for one they could have had, seven for the life they never did.

Seven minutes was all that they got at the end.
